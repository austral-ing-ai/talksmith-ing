## Key claims
Relevance for the Talk: this is a **published recipe for training a reasoning on/off switch and a thinking-budget cap into one model with SFT data design**, not with a special RL reward. Reasoning-off comes from SFT samples with the trace removed. Budget robustness comes from SFT samples whose trace was cut short but whose final answer was kept. The budget itself is enforced at inference by the serving code, which inserts `</think>`; the model is not told the budget.

### Reasoning control (the focus)
- **Reasoning-off mode is taught in Stage 1 SFT by stripping traces (§3.2, verbatim):** "Stage 1 uses the full dataset described in Section 3.1, augmented with a subsample of roughly 10% of prompts paired with outputs stripped of reasoning traces. This exposes the model to “empty” traces, enabling it to produce direct answers in a reasoning-off mode."
- **Budget robustness is taught in Stage 3 SFT by truncating traces (§3.2, verbatim):** "Stage 3 reinforces long-context capability. It incorporates long-context data following the recipe used in Nemotron-H preparation (NVIDIA, 2025), along with augmented examples across domains where reasoning traces were abruptly truncated to 1–2k tokens while preserving the final answer. This truncation strategy improved robustness under varying inference-time thinking budgets."
- **Size of the truncated share (§1, verbatim):** "About 5% of the data contained deliberately truncated reasoning traces, enabling fine-grained thinking budget control at inference time (§3.4)." The same paragraph lists "truncated (budgeted) training" among the targeted SFT areas. (See Inconsistencies: 5% of which data is not stated.)
- **RL also sees both modes (§3.2 RLHF, verbatim):** "we use GRPO to train candidate checkpoints from the SFT stage using English-only contexts from HelpSteer3 (Wang et al., 2025). During training, we generate responses both with and without thinking traces and use a Qwen-based reward model to judge the rollouts."
- **How the budget is enforced at inference (§3.4, verbatim):** "This feature is implemented by counting tokens after the model begins generating the `<think>` token. Once the budget is reached, the inference setup attempts to insert a closing `</think>` tag. Rather than inserting it immediately, we let the model finish its current sentence and place the tag at the next newline. In extreme cases where no newline appears, the system enforces closure within 500 tokens past the budget: if no newline occurs by the (budget+500)th token, the `</think>` tag is forcibly inserted."
  - What counts: only thinking tokens ("how many thinking tokens the model may generate before producing the final answer"). "The final answer is the portion of text typically shown to end users."
  - The budget is enforced by "the inference setup", not by the model reading a number. Nothing in the paper puts the budget into the prompt (checked: no mention of a budget instruction, system prompt, or prompt text anywhere in `page.md`).
- **Failure mode 1: the answer "compensates" (§3.4, verbatim):** "In one failure mode, the model uses more tokens in the final answer to “compensate” for restrictions in the thinking traces. Without truncated training examples in the SFT stage, this compensation effect is prevalent (Figure 5a, center). With truncated training, however, the effect is absent (Figure 5b, center)."
- **Failure mode 2: the model keeps thinking after the forced `</think>` (§3.4, verbatim):** "Another issue is that the model can remain in “thinking mode” even after the closing tag `</think>` is inserted. This is evident when the model generates the closing tag again after the forced insertion, suggesting it does not fully “register” the artificial closure. We evaluate this using “Well-Formedness,” where a well-formed response should contain only a single closing tag (either forced by the budget or produced naturally). Figure 5a (right) shows that for short budgets, the percentage of well-formed responses drops sharply. With truncation training, however, the model consistently produces well-formed responses (Figure 5b, right)."
- **Figure 5 caption (verbatim):** "Figure 5: Comparison of budget control before truncation training (a) and after truncation training was included (b). For all plots above the x-axis indicates the budget assigned for thinking tokens."
- **Evaluation is reported with reasoning ON** (Table 8 caption: "Evaluation results with reasoning "ON""). No reasoning-off accuracy numbers are reported in the paper.

### Model and pipeline (context)
- **What it is (Abstract):** "Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2, a hybrid Mamba-Transformer language model designed to increase throughput for reasoning workloads while achieving state-of-the-art accuracy compared to similarly-sized models." It "builds on the Nemotron-H architecture, in which the majority of the self-attention layers in the common Transformer architecture are replaced with Mamba-2 layers, to achieve improved inference speed when generating the long thinking traces needed for reasoning."
- **Headline throughput (Abstract):** "Compared to existing similarly-sized models (e.g., Qwen3-8B), we show that Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 achieves on-par or better accuracy on reasoning benchmarks while achieving up to 6× higher inference throughput in reasoning settings like 8k input and 16k output tokens". Figure 1 caption: "up to 6.3× higher throughput". Intro: "3×–6× higher throughput than Qwen3-8B ... for generation-heavy scenarios like 1k input / 8k output or 8k input / 16k output tokens".
- **Lineage:** pre-train Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base on 20T tokens (FP8) → long-context phase (512k CPT) → align the 12B (SFT 1–3, GRPO, DPO, RLHF, merging) → prune + distill to 9B (Minitron) → re-run DPO, GRPO, KD, RLHF, merging on the 9B. Released: NVIDIA-Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 ("the aligned and pruned reasoning model"), NVIDIA-Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2-Base, NVIDIA-Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base.
- **Post-training size (§1):** "Overall, post-training was performed on roughly 90 billion tokens, the majority in single-turn prompt–response format with reasoning traces." §3.1: "a large-scale SFT stage which trains the base model on approximately 80 billion tokens of prompt-response pairs."
- **Where the reasoning traces come from (§3.1):** Math, science and coding responses generated with "the open-weights DeepSeek-R1-0528 model"; conversational responses from "the Qwen3-235B-A22B reasoning model" (LMSYS, HelpSteer2/3, ~550k WildChat-1M prompts) plus multi-turn DeepSeek R1 responses; tool-calling with Qwen3-235B-A22B playing User-Agent, Assistant-Agent and API-Server-Agent; safety responses from DeepSeek-R1-0528 filtered by guard models.
- **Stage 2 SFT (tool calling, verbatim):** "Although Stage 1 improved performance on most benchmarks, tool-calling accuracy degraded. We attribute this to sample concatenation at 128k, which likely disrupted learning of tool-calling patterns. Thus, Stage 2 was trained without concatenation, using the full tool-calling dataset and a representative subsample of other domains."
- **Stage 1 packing:** "we concatenate samples into sequences of approximately 128k tokens, reducing padding overhead and encouraging long-range learning."
- **IFEval RL:** 16,000 LMSYS prompts "augmented ... with IFEval-style instructions"; "A rule-based verifier scored outputs".
- **DPO for tool calling:** iterative on-policy DPO in the WorkBench environment, "positive examples (successful tool calls) and negative examples (failed generations)".
- **Model merging (§3.2):** "we observed a trade-off between reasoning capabilities and chat capabilities ... blending in an RL checkpoint with strong reasoning capabilities with an RL checkpoint with strong chat capabilities ... linearly interpolating model weights: (1−α)·w_model1 + α·w_model2 ... values around 0.5 offered a good trade-off." (This α is unrelated to any budget parameter.)
- **The 9B reasoning model saw the Stage 3 (truncated) data during distillation (§4.3, verbatim):** "We observe that a mix of 70% post-training stage 2 data (Section 3.2) and 30% pretraining (Section 2.2) data yields the highest accuracy (Table 11). For KD at sequence length 262,144, we use 100% stage 3 post-training data (Section 3.2)."
- **Compression target (§4):** "storing just the weights of a 12B parameter model in bfloat16 precision requires 22.9 GiB, which is more than the 22 GiB memory capacity of an A10G GPU". Memory budget 19.66 GiB for 128k context, batch 1. Final 9B: 56 layers (from 62), embedding 5120 → 4480, FFN 20480 → 15680, Mamba heads not pruned (Candidate 2: 128 heads, 8.89B params).
- **Architecture (§2.1):** 62 layers: 6 self-attention, 28 FFN, 28 Mamba-2; hidden 5120; FFN 20480; GQA 40 query / 8 KV heads; squared ReLU; no position embeddings; RMSNorm. The 9B keeps 4 attention layers ("attention-to-total-layers ratio between 7-8% is reasonable").

## Definitions and terminology
- **Reasoning-off mode**: the model produces "direct answers" with an "empty" thinking trace. Trained by Stage 1 SFT samples whose outputs were "stripped of reasoning traces".
- **"Empty" traces**: the paper's term (in quotes in the source) for those stripped outputs. The paper does not show the token-level form (see Inconsistencies; the model card's chat template emits `<think></think>`).
- **Truncation training / truncated (budgeted) training**: SFT on examples "where reasoning traces were abruptly truncated to 1–2k tokens while preserving the final answer".
- **Thinking budget**: "how many thinking tokens the model may generate before producing the final answer"; counted "after the model begins generating the `<think>` token".
- **Final answer**: "the portion of text typically shown to end users" (the text after `</think>`).
- **budget+500 rule**: if no newline appears by token budget+500, `</think>` "is forcibly inserted".
- **Compensation effect**: the model "uses more tokens in the final answer to “compensate” for restrictions in the thinking traces".
- **Well-Formedness**: "a well-formed response should contain only a single closing tag (either forced by the budget or produced naturally)". A response that emits `</think>` again after the forced one is not well-formed.
- **Nemotron-H**: the earlier NVIDIA hybrid Mamba-Transformer family (arXiv 2504.03624) this model builds on.
- **Minitron**: NVIDIA's pruning + distillation framework, "extend[ed] ... to compress reasoning models".
- **WSD**: Warmup-Stable-Decay learning-rate schedule. **KD**: knowledge distillation (forward-KL, logit-based). **ISL / OSL**: input / output sequence length.
- **Stage 1 / 2 / 3 SFT**: full data + ~10% reasoning-stripped, packed to 128k / tool calling, unpacked / long context + truncated traces.

## Inconsistencies / open questions
- [verified] **The paper never documents the user-facing on/off switch.** `page.md` and `original.html` contain no occurrence of `/no_think`, "system prompt", or a reasoning-off instruction string; the only `/think` hits are the `</think>` tag in §3.4 (grep of both files). How a user or training sample *requests* reasoning-off is documented only in the model card (`/think` / `/no_think` in the system or user message; see `nvidia-nemotron-nano-9b-v2-card.web.md`). The paper says only that stripped samples enable "a reasoning-off mode".
- [open question] **The token-level format of the Stage 1 "empty" traces is not documented.** The paper does not say whether a stripped sample keeps an empty `<think></think>` block, drops the tags, or carries a `/no_think` flag in the prompt. The model card's chat template emits `<think></think>` at the start of the assistant turn when `/no_think` is set, which suggests that form, but that is inference behaviour, not a training-data statement. Settled by the released post-training dataset (Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset-v1/v2) or NVIDIA's data code.
- [open question] **The exact format of the truncated Stage 3 samples is not documented.** The paper says only "reasoning traces were abruptly truncated to 1–2k tokens while preserving the final answer". It does not say: where the cut falls (mid-sentence, as "abruptly" suggests, or at a newline, as the inference rule does); whether `</think>` is placed right after the cut; whether any bridging text is added (the card's client appends "." before `</think>`); how the 1–2k length was chosen per sample (uniform? fixed?); whether the "preserved" final answer is the original answer written after the full trace, reused verbatim; and whether the prompt signals anything. Settled by the released dataset or training code.
- [open question] **"About 5% of the data" — 5% of what?** §1 says "About 5% of the data contained deliberately truncated reasoning traces" right after "post-training was performed on roughly 90 billion tokens". §3.2 places the truncated examples in Stage 3 only. Whether 5% is of all ~90B post-training tokens, of samples, or of the Stage 3 mix is not stated. Settled by the authors.
- [open question] **The budgets tested vs the 1–2k truncation range.** Truncation was to 1–2k tokens, while §3.4 says budget control is robust at "varying inference-time thinking budgets" and Figure 5a shows well-formedness dropping "for short budgets". The x-axis values of Figure 5 are not given in the text (image transcription pending). Whether budgets below 1k were evaluated, and how well control extrapolates outside 1–2k, is settled by reading the Figure 5 panels (Phase 2) or the authors.
- [open question] **Which model Figure 5 shows (12B or 9B).** §3 is about the 12B aligned checkpoint ("Our 12B model’s performance is summarized in Table 8"), but §3.4 says "Nemotron Nano V2" without a size. The 9B also received Stage 3 data during distillation (§4.3). Settled by the authors.
- [open question] **What the left panel of Figure 5 plots.** The text names only the center (final-answer length, "compensation") and right (Well-Formedness) panels, plus "Apart from just presenting the accuracy of the model at various budgets". The left panel is presumably accuracy vs budget, but the text does not say so. Settled by Phase 2 transcription of the images.
- [verified] **Budget enforcement in the paper vs the card's reference client differ.** The paper's rule: close at the next newline after the budget, forcibly at budget+500. The model card's `max_thinking_tokens` description repeats that rule, but the card's `ThinkingBudgetClient` code stops the first call at `max_tokens=max_thinking_budget` and, if `</think>` is absent, appends `".\n</think>\n\n"` immediately (no newline wait, no +500 window). Which mechanism produced Figure 5 is not stated; "the inference setup" suggests the newline rule.
- [verified] **Two token totals for post-training.** §1: "roughly 90 billion tokens" (all post-training); §3.1: "approximately 80 billion tokens" (the large-scale SFT stage). Compatible (the SFT stage is part of post-training), but a slide should say which one it quotes. Checked in `page.md`.

### Abstract
"We introduce Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2, a hybrid Mamba-Transformer language model designed to increase throughput for reasoning workloads while achieving state-of-the-art accuracy compared to similarly-sized models. Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 builds on the Nemotron-H architecture, in which the majority of the self-attention layers in the common Transformer architecture are replaced with Mamba-2 layers, to achieve improved inference speed when generating the long thinking traces needed for reasoning. We create Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 by first pre-training a 12-billion-parameter model (Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base) on 20 trillion tokens using an FP8 training recipe. After aligning Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base, we employ the Minitron strategy to compress and distill the model with the goal of enabling inference on up to 128k tokens on a single NVIDIA A10G GPU (22GiB of memory, bfloat16 precision). Compared to existing similarly-sized models (e.g., Qwen3-8B), we show that Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 achieves on-par or better accuracy on reasoning benchmarks while achieving up to 6× higher inference throughput in reasoning settings like 8k input and 16k output tokens (Figure 1). We are releasing Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2, Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base, and Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2-Base checkpoints along with the majority of our pre- and post-training datasets on Hugging Face."

### §1 Introduction (post-training paragraph)
"Nemotron Nano 2 was then post-trained through a combination of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023), and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Christiano et al., 2017). We applied multiple SFT stages across various domains, followed by targeted SFT on key areas such as tool use, long-context performance, and truncated (budgeted) training. GRPO and RLHF sharpened instruction-following and conversational ability, while additional DPO stages further strengthened tool use. Overall, post-training was performed on roughly 90 billion tokens, the majority in single-turn prompt–response format with reasoning traces. About 5% of the data contained deliberately truncated reasoning traces, enabling fine-grained thinking budget control at inference time (§3.4)."

### §3.2 Post Training (full)
"**Stage 1 SFT.** As Figure 4 illustrates, we employ three distinct stages of supervised fine-tuning. Stage 1 uses the full dataset described in Section 3.1, augmented with a subsample of roughly 10% of prompts paired with outputs stripped of reasoning traces. This exposes the model to “empty” traces, enabling it to produce direct answers in a reasoning-off mode. To improve efficiency and preserve long-context ability from pretraining, we concatenate samples into sequences of approximately 128k tokens, reducing padding overhead and encouraging long-range learning.

**Stage 2 SFT.** Stage 2 targets tool-calling. Although Stage 1 improved performance on most benchmarks, tool-calling accuracy degraded. We attribute this to sample concatenation at 128k, which likely disrupted learning of tool-calling patterns. Thus, Stage 2 was trained without concatenation, using the full tool-calling dataset and a representative subsample of other domains.

**Stage 3 SFT.** Stage 3 reinforces long-context capability. It incorporates long-context data following the recipe used in Nemotron-H preparation (NVIDIA, 2025), along with augmented examples across domains where reasoning traces were abruptly truncated to 1–2k tokens while preserving the final answer. This truncation strategy improved robustness under varying inference-time thinking budgets.

**IFeval RL.** To improve instruction adherence, we sampled 16,000 prompts from the LMSYS Chat dataset and augmented them with IFEval-style instructions. A rule-based verifier scored outputs based on how well they satisfied each instruction, creating a reward signal that prioritized following directions with precision. IFEval RL experiments provided significant boost to IFEval capabilities while the rest of the benchmarks fluctuated slightly requiring careful checkpoint selection.

**DPO.** In another branch of training, we apply the DPO algorithm to improve tool-calling. We evaluate performance using the BFCL v3 benchmark, which extends BFCL v2 with greater emphasis on multi-step (multiple tool calls to achieve a goal) and multi-turn (multiple user–agent interactions). To strengthen these capabilities in the Nano V2 aligned model, we use the WorkBench environment, a multi-step verifiable tool-calling setup adapted from Styles (Styles et al., 2024). In each WorkBench task, the model must issue a sequence of tool calls across multiple steps, with correctness verified through database state comparisons.

Nano V2 undergoes reinforcement learning in this environment through iterative stages of Direct Preference Optimization. For each candidate checkpoint from the long-context stage, we generate on-policy data consisting of positive examples (successful tool calls) and negative examples (failed generations) for every WorkBench prompt. This process ensures that iterative DPO remains on-policy.

**RLHF.** We evaluate the model’s overall helpfulness and chat capabilities using the Arena-Hard benchmark. To improve performance on this benchmark, we use GRPO to train candidate checkpoints from the SFT stage using English-only contexts from HelpSteer3 (Wang et al., 2025). During training, we generate responses both with and without thinking traces and use a Qwen-based reward model to judge the rollouts.

**Model Merging.** During training, we observed a trade-off between reasoning capabilities and chat capabilities. To address this, we opted for checkpoint interpolation Wortsman et al. (2022), blending in an RL checkpoint with strong reasoning capabilities with an RL checkpoint with strong chat capabilities. Checkpoint interpolation is performed by linearly interpolating model weights: (1−α)·w_model1 + α·w_model2. We experimented with a parameter sweep over α values from 0.1 to 0.9 in increments of 0.1, and found that values around 0.5 offered a good trade-off."

### §3.4 Budget Control Evaluation (full)
"Nemotron Nano V2 allows users to specify how many thinking tokens the model may generate before producing the final answer. The final answer is the portion of text typically shown to end users. This feature is implemented by counting tokens after the model begins generating the `<think>` token. Once the budget is reached, the inference setup attempts to insert a closing `</think>` tag. Rather than inserting it immediately, we let the model finish its current sentence and place the tag at the next newline. In extreme cases where no newline appears, the system enforces closure within 500 tokens past the budget: if no newline occurs by the (budget+500)th token, the `</think>` tag is forcibly inserted.

[Figure 5 (a) badbudget.png, (b) budget.png]
Figure 5: Comparison of budget control before truncation training (a) and after truncation training was included (b). For all plots above the x-axis indicates the budget assigned for thinking tokens.

Figure 5b shows our models budget control behavior. Apart from just presenting the accuracy of the model at various budgets, we also inspect if the model generations are well-formatted at various budgets. We inspect for two kinds of failure modes:

• In one failure mode, the model uses more tokens in the final answer to “compensate” for restrictions in the thinking traces. Without truncated training examples in the SFT stage, this compensation effect is prevalent (Figure 5a, center). With truncated training, however, the effect is absent (Figure 5b, center).

• Another issue is that the model can remain in “thinking mode” even after the closing tag `</think>` is inserted. This is evident when the model generates the closing tag again after the forced insertion, suggesting it does not fully “register” the artificial closure. We evaluate this using “Well-Formedness,” where a well-formed response should contain only a single closing tag (either forced by the budget or produced naturally). Figure 5a (right) shows that for short budgets, the percentage of well-formed responses drops sharply. With truncation training, however, the model consistently produces well-formed responses (Figure 5b, right)."

### §4.3 Retraining with Distillation (reasoning model)

### §5 Conclusion
"In this report, we introduced Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2, a hybrid Mamba-Transformer reasoning model that achieves comparable or better accuracies at up to 6× higher throughput than existing state-of-the-art models such as Qwen3-8B. To create Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2, we started by pre-training Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base on 20T tokens, using a carefully constructed mix of curated and synthetically generated data. We aligned Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base using several stages of SFT, GRPO, DPO, and RLHF before using the Minitron compression via pruning and distillation strategy to produce the final model. As a result of this compression, Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 can run inference on context lengths of up to 128k tokens in bfloat16 precision on a single NVIDIA A10G GPU with 22 GiB of memory. We have open-sourced Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 along with its corresponding sibling Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2-Base and parent Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2-Base models, plus the majority of its pre- and post-training data on HuggingFace (links at the bottom of Section 1)."
