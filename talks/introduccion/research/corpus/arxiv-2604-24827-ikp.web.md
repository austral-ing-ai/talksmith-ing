# Incompressible Knowledge Probes: Estimating Black-Box LLM Parameter Counts via Factual Capacity

## Provenance

- Original location: research/web/arxiv-2604-24827-ikp/
- Format: arXiv abstract page (HTML)
- Author / source (if known): Bojie Li
- Date of original (if known): submitted 27 April 2026; revised 5 July 2026 (v2)

## Key claims

- The paper introduces Incompressible Knowledge Probes (IKPs), a benchmark of 1,400 factual questions across seven obscurity tiers for estimating the parameter count of black-box LLMs from factual recall.
- It calibrates a log-linear relationship using 93 open-weight models from 135M to 1,600B parameters across 19 vendors, reporting R² = 0.910.
- The paper frames the output as order-of-magnitude effective capacity and relative ranking, explicitly not as a precise parameter count; its 90% prediction interval is roughly a factor of three in either direction.
- For Mixture-of-Experts models, total parameters correlate better with the factual-capacity measure than active parameters (reported R² 0.67 vs. 0.41).
- It evaluates 201 models from 27 vendors; estimates for safety-tuned closed models are lower bounds when refusals reduce observable recall.

## Definitions and terminology

- Incompressible Knowledge Probe (IKP): a factual question intended to test knowledge that cannot be derived through reasoning or compressed through architectural improvements.
- Factual capacity: the observable store of factual knowledge used here as a coarse lower-bound signal for model size.
- Prediction interval: the uncertainty band around a calibrated estimate; it is materially wider than a point estimate implies.

## Evidence and examples

- Abstract validation: median leave-one-out fold error 1.48×; 72% of held-out models within 2×; 86% within 3×.
- Paper identifier: arXiv:2604.24827, CS.LG / CS.AI, released under CC BY 4.0.

## Inconsistencies / open questions

- The captured source is the arXiv abstract page, not the full PDF or experimental HTML manuscript; the PDF link is preserved in the raw capture.
- The method estimates an effective capacity signal, not proprietary model architecture, active MoE parameters, training compute or deployment economics.
- Tool use, retrieval, refusals and release-date effects can affect observed factual recall and should be discussed when using the result pedagogically.
